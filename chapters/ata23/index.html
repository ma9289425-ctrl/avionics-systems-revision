Communication Systems – Overview

Core Revision

Primary system purposes retained in the revision notes.

Large-aircraft communication systems connect the flight crew with air traffic control, other aircraft, ground operations, the passenger cabin, ground personnel and satellite services.

ATC communication

Communication between the flight crew and air traffic control.

Air-to-air communication

Communication between aircraft while in flight.

Ground operations

Communication with operational personnel.

Flight deck ↔ cabin

Interphone communication between the flight deck and passenger cabin.

Flight deck ↔ ground crew

Interphone communication with personnel outside the aircraft.

Satellite services

Operational communication and passenger internet through SATCOM.

System view

02Primary ATC voice link

VHF Radio

Core Revision

Primary ATC voice-link facts and values.

VHF = Very High Frequency

VHF maintains radio voice communication between aircraft and air traffic control and also supports air-to-air communication.

Frequency range118.0–136.975MHz
Channel spacing25kHz
Designated channels720Separate and distinct channels
Typical minimum2 radiosFor redundancy

Operating characteristics

  • Relatively short range.
  • Requires line-of-sight between stations.
  • Used for aircraft ↔ ATC and air-to-air communication.
  • Each party transmits and receives on the same channel.
  • Only one party can transmit at any one time.
Source Detail

Typical transport-aircraft installation

  • Radio typically located in an avionics bay.
  • Connected to a flight-deck tuning and frequency display head.
  • Connected to the flight-deck audio control panel through a data bus.
1

Key point

On the selected VHF channel, only one party can transmit at a time.

04Incoming-call alert

SELCAL

Core Revision

Incoming-call alert used with VHF or HF.

SELCAL = Selective Calling

SELCAL works in conjunction with VHF or HF radio and alerts the flight crew when ground operations calls the aircraft, without requiring continuous radio monitoring.

1

Ground call

Ground operations contacts the aircraft through VHF or HF.

2

Visual indication

The SELCAL panel on the flight deck illuminates.

3

Audible indication

A tone is heard to alert the flight crew.

🔔

Recall association

SELCAL = incoming-call alert by light + tone.

08Internal & ground communication

Service Interphone

Core Revision

Internal and ground communication links.

The service interphone is an intercom-type system linking the flight deck, passenger cabin and personnel outside the aircraft.

Flight deck↔Passenger cabin
Flight deck↔Ground personnel
Passenger cabin↔Ground personnel

Typical exterior jack areas

Fueling stationCargo baysWheel wellsEquipment baysEnginesAPU area

Ground crew call system

  • Creates audible and visual alerts on the flight deck when ground crew wish to speak.
  • Allows flight-deck personnel to notify ground crew to connect to the interphone.
  • A horn on the external APU control panel sounds to gain ground-crew attention.
Source Detail

Additional operation and installation detail

  • Cabin phones become active when picked up.
  • A switch must be operated to broadcast over the cabin speaker system.
  • Flight-deck crew can typically choose between the passenger cabin and ground crew.
  • Maintenance personnel connect through exterior headset jacks.
  • The original location list also specifies the tail cone, engine nacelles, APU compartment and APU control panel.
  • Modern systems are controlled by an audio management unit or similar equipment-bay unit, are DC powered and often incorporate the ground crew call system.
Manufacturer-Specific NoteBoeing detail in the supplied source: the same external APU-panel horn is also described as indicating an equipment-cooling failure and sounding when the air data inertial reference unit is on while no AC power is available.
09Search-and-rescue beacon

Emergency Locator Transmitter (ELT)

Core Revision

Emergency activation, location signal, installation, controls and maintenance.

ELT = Emergency Locator Transmitter

An ELT is an independent battery-powered transmitter activated by excessive G-forces during a crash. Its signal is received by satellite systems and relayed for search-and-rescue action.

Digital frequency406.025MHz
Power5 WSource value
Operating timeAt least 24 hMinimum duration
Automatic triggerCrash G-forceExcessive G-forces
406.025MHz ELT signal

Typical installation and controls

  • Typically installed as far aft in the fuselage as practicable, just forward of the empennage.
  • Built-in G-force sensor aligned with the aircraft's longitudinal axis.
  • Flight-deck panel alerts the pilot if activated.
  • Flight-deck controls permit ARM, TEST and manual activation.
ARM
TEST
MANUAL

Core maintenance checks

  • Proper installation
  • Battery condition
  • Operation of controls
  • Crash-sensor operation
  • Sufficient signal at the antenna
!

Maintenance caution

Do not accidentally activate the ELT and transmit an emergency distress signal.

Source Detail

Inspection and records

  • Inspect within 12 months of the previous inspection.
  • Check for battery corrosion as well as the controls, crash sensor and antenna signal.
  • Record the inspection and new battery-expiration date in maintenance records and on the outside of the ELT.
  • Built-in test equipment facilitates testing without transmitting an emergency signal.
Source Detail

Identification and portability

  • The digital signal contains unique registration information identifying the aircraft, owner and contact information.
  • Automatic G-force units are easily removable and often contain a portable antenna.
  • ELTs are equipped with multidirectional activation devices.
Specific Source NoteIntentional signal activation: only from the top of each hour to five minutes after the hour, for no longer than three audible sweeps. Contacting the local air traffic control tower before testing is recommended. Follow the ELT and airframe manufacturers' instructions.
10Crash-protected recorders

CVR and FDR – “Black Boxes”

Core Revision

Two separate recorders with different content and storage.

The common name “black box” refers to two separate pieces of equipment: the Cockpit Voice Recorder and the Flight Data Recorder. Their information is typically used after an accident to help investigators determine its cause.

CVR

Cockpit Voice Recorder

Records flight-crew conversations, radio communications and cockpit sounds.

Maximum storage2 hours
At the limitOverwrites older material
Primary contentConversation and cockpit audio
Recorder typeSeparate from the FDR
FDR

Flight Data Recorder

Records critical flight and operational parameters, including engine conditions, air data, flight controls, navigation and hydraulic-system data.

ParametersAbout 1,000
Storage25 hours
ExamplesAltitude, airspeed, temperature
Recorder typeSeparate from the CVR

International orange

Both recorders are painted international orange for easier identification.

Reflective tape

Reflective strips help locate the recorders if they are underwater.

Underwater acoustic beacon

An underwater acoustic beacon is attached to assist location.

Source Detail

Operation and synchronisation

  • The CVR uses several microphones and records ambient cockpit noises, including a possible malfunction.
  • A time signal is transmitted to the CVR for synchronisation.
  • The FDR automatically starts when one engine is running and stops 5 minutes after the last engine shuts down.
  • The underwater acoustic beacon is battery powered, with an expected battery life of 6 years.
