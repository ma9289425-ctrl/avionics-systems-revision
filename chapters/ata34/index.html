01Radio NavigationVOR, ADF, DME and RNAV⌄

VOR

  • Ground station transmits 360° radials; the receiver shows bearing information, course deviation and TO/FROM.
  • OBS selects the course; CDI shows lateral deviation.
  • VOT: tune 108.0 MHz, center CDI, expect 0° FROM or 180° TO; error must not exceed ±4°.

ADF / NDB

  • Loop antenna senses direction; the sense antenna removes 180° ambiguity.
  • A movable-card indicator can show magnetic bearing to the station.
  • Correct antenna alignment with the aircraft longitudinal axis is important.

DME

  • Operates in 962-1213 MHz and calculates distance from pulse travel time.
  • Normally displays slant distance, which exceeds ground distance when the aircraft is high and close to the station.
  • DME HOLD keeps one DME station while the VOR frequency is changed.

RNAV

  • Allows point-to-point navigation without directly overflying ground aids.
  • Waypoints may use coordinates or a VOR radial plus distance.
  • VOR-based RNAV can create calculated “phantom” waypoints.

Correction rule

150 Hz dominance moves the localizer CDI left, so correct left. 90 Hz dominance moves it right, so correct right.

Flight director

It computes and displays the required attitude with command bars. The pilot follows the commands; an autopilot moves the controls automatically.

03FMS, GPS, WAAS & IRSManagement, satellite position and inertial reference⌄

FMS

The FMC is the main computer; the CDU is the flight-deck interface. The FMS coordinates navigation and other aircraft systems through data buses and includes BITE monitoring.

GPS

Three satellites provide a two-dimensional fix. A fourth adds altitude for a three-dimensional fix. The source describes 24 satellites: 21 active and 3 spares.

WAAS

Approximately 25 surveyed ground stations generate corrections that are retransmitted through geostationary satellites. The source states 1-3 m lateral and vertical error.

INS / IRS

Self-contained navigation calculates position from acceleration after initialization. Modern IRS uses three ring-laser gyros and three solid-state accelerometers; GPS can initialize and correct it.

04Surveillance & Collision AvoidanceTransponder, TCAS and ADS-B⌄

ATC transponder

  • Ground interrogation: 1030 MHz; aircraft reply: 1090 MHz.
  • 4,096 selectable codes; IDENT highlights the target.
  • ALT / Mode C transmits pressure altitude from an altitude encoder.

TCAS

  • TA = locate and assess nearby traffic.
  • RA = specific vertical evasive command.
  • Two TCAS II aircraft can coordinate opposite vertical actions.

ADS-B OUT

Combines GPS-derived position with altitude, velocity and time, then broadcasts it to other equipped aircraft and ground stations.

ADS-B IN

Receives traffic or ground-provided information and displays it to the flight crew. Accurate ADS-B testing is essential because separation depends on correct data.

05Weather Detection & Radio AltimeterRadar, lightning, uplinked weather and AGL height⌄

Weather radar

  • Stronger precipitation gives stronger returns.
  • Typical coding: green light, yellow medium, red heavy, magenta intense/extreme.
  • Clouds may give no return; dry-air turbulence is not detectable.

Radar safety

Do not look into a transmitting antenna. Follow approved radome, hangar, building and refuelling precautions; maintenance is for qualified personnel.

Lightning / uplink

Lightning systems estimate azimuth with a loop antenna and range from signal intensity. Remote weather can be sent by satellite or ground link for wider coverage.

Radio altimeter

Measures height above terrain directly below the aircraft, mainly below 2,500 ft. It is more responsive than pressure altitude for low-level AGL information.

06ACARSAircraft-ground digital data link⌄

Purpose

ACARS exchanges digital messages between aircraft and ground stations. It began with VHF and may also use SATCOM.

CMU

The Communications Management Unit routes external data and may select the most efficient available air-ground link.

Message groups

Air Traffic Control, Aeronautical Operational Control, and Airline Administrative Control.

Example use

Updated arrival information can be sent during descent; a touchdown report can be sent automatically to dispatch.

07Avionics Installation & TestingMounting, bonding, BITE and test equipment⌄

Installation

Secure mounting, clearance, cooling and moisture protection are essential. Shock mounts isolate vibration; non-conductive mounts may require a bonding jumper.

BITE

Continuously checks parameters, detects and helps isolate faults, and can store or transmit maintenance information. It does not replace all external testing.

General instruments

Multimeter: voltage/current/resistance. Oscilloscope: voltage against time. TDR: cable open/short and distance to fault.

Testing rule

Use manufacturer procedures and calibrated equipment. Test accuracy must be equal to or better than the accuracy required for the equipment under test.
